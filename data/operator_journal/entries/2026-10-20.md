## 2. The benchmark reality check (empirical, this session)

I could not run the repo's own backtest here (`yfinance` is not installed in
this sandbox). Instead I pulled **real SPY price history via the IBKR MCP
connector** (contract 756733, monthly bars, 2026-10-20) and computed buy-and-hold
returns:

| Window | SPY buy-and-hold | Bot best documented backtest |
|---|---|---|
| ~13 mo (Oct 2024 → Nov 2025) | **+20.2% total / +18.5% ann.** | — |
| 2 yr (Oct 2024 → Oct 2026) | **+34.8% total / +16.1% ann.** | +6.9% total / **+3.4% ann.** |
| Since bot went stale (Mar → Oct 2026) | **+17.9%** | (bot idle) |

**SPY buy-and-hold returned ~4.7x the bot's best backtested annualized return**,
with zero execution risk, zero commission churn, and no shorting into a bull
market. And the bot's 3.4% figure is *optimistic*: the backtest runs on **daily
bars** and by its own docstring "cannot simulate" the intraday VWAP and
first-hour logic the live bot actually trades, and it is quoted before honest
costs (commission, slippage, spread, short borrow). Real net results would be
lower, not higher.

> Note: the documented backtest window (Feb 2024 – Nov 2025) slightly predates my
> SPY data (starts Oct 2024). The conclusion is insensitive to this — SPY was
> up strongly across every sub-window of the period.

## 3. Assessment

The mission is explicit and uncompromising: **real P&L, net of honest costs,
that beats SPY buy-and-hold.** On every piece of evidence available:

1. The strategy's own math caps it near 7%/yr (38% WR, 1.29 PF). SPY did ~16%/yr.
2. There is no live evidence it even reaches that cap (2 outcomes).
3. It has been positioned almost entirely **short in a bull market.**
4. The documented "path to profit" quietly substitutes selling signals and
   adding leverage for actual trading edge.

No single gate tweak, feature add, or threshold change closes a ~4.7x gap to a
passive index. The honest bottleneck is not a bug in a filter — it is that an
intraday RRS momentum strategy has not demonstrated an edge over passive
indexing, and its own analysis says it cannot without changing what "winning"
means (selling signals / taking leverage).

This is exactly the situation the mandate names: *"If the evidence keeps saying
no strategy works, say so and recommend escalation or wind-down."*

## 4. What I decided NOT to do (and why)

- **Did not raise risk / apply the "aggressive" config.** `QUICK_START_100X`'s
  3% risk, 20% position config amplifies drawdown on a sub-benchmark strategy.
  Prohibited in spirit by the mandate; counterproductive on the merits.
- **Did not touch `risk/`.** No changes there this session.
- **Did not add more filters/features.** The strategy already discards ~98% of
  signals; more gates lower trade count, not the return ceiling.
- **Did not fabricate a backtest.** I reported the documented numbers and a
  real, independently-sourced SPY benchmark, and labeled the fidelity caveats.

## 5. Recommendation to the human (decision needed)

Pick a direction deliberately rather than drifting:

- **(A) Disciplined falsification program.** Keep paper-only. Define *up front*
  the bar: "a configuration beats SPY buy-and-hold, net of honestly-modeled
  costs, out-of-sample, across ≥2 regimes." Then run the existing
  walk-forward/research harness **with SPY buy-and-hold as the mandatory
  benchmark column and realistic cost modeling** (the current harness reports
  absolute $ P&L with no benchmark and no spread/borrow costs — that is how a
  3.4% strategy looks like "success"). If nothing clears the bar after honest
  effort, that is a definitive negative result.
- **(B) Wind down the trading ambition.** Relabel the project as paper/research
  and stop implying a path to live profitability. If the real goal is a
  signal-service *business*, name it as such — it is a product/marketing effort,
  not a trading edge, and should be evaluated on customer willingness-to-pay,
  not conflated with trading P&L.

My read of the evidence favors starting with **(A)** but with a hard
pre-committed stopping rule, precisely so it does not become another open-ended
optimization that never confronts the benchmark. I did not choose for the human;
this is a capital-and-time decision that belongs to them.
