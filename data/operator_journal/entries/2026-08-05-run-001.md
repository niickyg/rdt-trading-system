## Assessment
The mission is "beat SPY buy-and-hold, net of honest costs." Measured against that bar:

1. **The bot loses to SPY by ~5x, gross.** Config C (the repo's best) = +6.9% over ~2yr
   (+3.4%/yr). SPY buy-and-hold over the same Feb-2024→Nov-2025 window = **+34.5% price
   (~+19.7%/yr total)**, verified from live IBKR SPY data (see `BASELINE.md`).
2. **The gross number is itself optimistic.** Neither backtest engine models commission,
   slippage, or spread (grep-verified: no such terms; fills at exact signal price). A
   realistic cost drag on 279 trades (~$3–6/trade) eats roughly half to all of the $1,716
   gross profit. Net return is plausibly **~0%**.
3. **No live evidence exists at all.** The connected account is empty ($5). Every claim of
   profitability rests on a cost-free daily-bar simulation of an intraday strategy.

So: **on current evidence the bot does not clear the bar, and it isn't close.** I am *not*
recommending wind-down on run 1 — that would be premature before a single honest net-of-cost
walk-forward has ever been run. But the burden of proof is now explicit and high (see
MANDATE §3), and the first thing to fix is that the measurement instrument itself is
dishonest (no costs).

Fairness note: the strategy's drawdown is far lower than SPY's (−$257 worst day vs SPY's
~−19% 2025 drawdown). A day-trading strategy's real pitch is uncorrelated, low-drawdown
returns, not out-returning a bull-market index. That's a legitimate lens — but it only
matters if the net return is clearly positive and the risk-adjusted return would tempt a
rational allocator away from SPY+cash. Right now we can't even show net-positive.
