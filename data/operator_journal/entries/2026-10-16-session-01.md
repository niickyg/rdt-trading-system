## 2. Assessment — is there honest evidence the bot beats SPY?

### 2a. The quoted results are gross of all costs
`grep` for `commission|slippage|fee|transaction_cost|spread` across `backtesting/`
returns **nothing**. The enhanced engine moved capital at entry/scale-out/close with no
cost deduction. So `CLAUDE.md`'s headline "RDT Filters: $1,716 (6.9%) over 2 years"
(3.4% annualized) is a **gross** figure.

### 2b. The project's own prior analysis already concedes a marginal/negative edge
`ACTIONABLE_100X_STRATEGY.md` (prior author) states: profit factor 1.29, win rate 38%,
and **Kelly criterion ≈ -0.02 (negative)**. Its own per-trade figures (avg win $70 ×
38% − avg loss $45 × 62%) imply **≈ -$1.3/trade expectancy before costs**. Its proposed
"path to 100%" pivots ~half the target to **selling a signal service** rather than
trading edge — which lines up with the most recent git commits being a "SaaS product
overhaul" (landing/pricing/login pages). The project has drifted from *proving a trading
edge* toward *productizing*.

### 2c. SPY buy-and-hold benchmark (authoritative, via IBKR)
SPY monthly closes, trailing 2 years: **568.64 (Oct 2024) → 771.79 (Oct 2026) = +35.7%
(~16.5% annualized).** Over the backtest's own cited window (Feb 2024–Nov 2025) it is
similar (~+36%). The bar the mandate sets is "beat this."

### 2d. Real-data net-vs-gross re-run (new, this session)
Using the project's own engine + direct yfinance data, Config-C **baseline** (no filters,
daily bars so no VWAP/first-hour), 8 liquid large-caps, 2024-09-16 → 2026-10-15:

| | Trades | Return $ | Return % | Win rate | Profit factor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Gross** (no costs) | 39 | **-$101.83** | -0.41% | 48.7% | 0.94 |
| **Net** (honest costs) | 39 | **-$282.96** | -1.13% | 46.2% | 0.84 |
| Txn costs paid | — | $175.08 (~$4.49/trade) | — | — | — |
| **SPY buy & hold** | — | **+$10,180** | **+40.72%** | — | — |

The strategy lost money **even gross**, in a strong bull market, on exactly the kind of
momentum names it is built to trade. Net of costs it loses ~2.8× more.

**Caveats (stated honestly):** this is the *baseline*, not the full 30-symbol RDT-filtered
Config C that the docs quote at +6.9% gross; it omits VWAP/first-hour (not simulable on
daily bars); and it's a small universe. So it is **not** a drop-in replacement for the
quoted number. But it is a real, reproducible demonstration that (i) costs are material
and were being ignored, and (ii) the signal has no obvious edge even where it should
shine. Applying the measured ~$4.49/trade to the full config's 279 trades (~$1,250) would
cut its quoted $1,716 gross to ~$466 net over 2 years ≈ **~0.9% annualized — still ~18×
worse than SPV's ~16.5%**.

## 3. What I changed (code)

**`backtesting/engine_enhanced.py`**
- Added cost params to `EnhancedBacktestEngine.__init__`: `commission_per_share=0.005`,
  `min_commission=1.0`, `slippage_bps=5.0` (ON by default → net results by default).
- Added `_execution_cost(price, shares)` helper (commission w/ per-order min + bps slippage).
- Applied costs at **entry**, every **scale-out**, and **close**; capital is reduced at
  each execution and the full accumulated cost is netted out of each trade's P&L once.
- Added `entry_cost` / `total_costs` to `EnhancedTrade`; `total_costs` to the result +
  `to_dict()`.

**`scripts/run_walkforward_v2.py`**
- Added matching entry cost to the custom `_enter_position_filtered` (exits inherit the
  engine's costed paths).
- Wired cost params through `BASE_ENGINE_CONFIG` → `make_engine_kwargs`.
- Output now labels results "NET of transaction costs" and prints a "Txn Costs Paid" row.

**Validation:** `py_compile` clean on both files. Unit test of the money mechanics
(enter 100@100 → scale 50@105 → close 50@110): gross $750, expected costs $13.375,
got **$13.375 exactly**; `final_capital − initial == Σ trade.pnl` (reconciles to 1e-9);
zero-cost toggle yields exactly the old behavior. Real-data run above exercised the full
path end-to-end.

**Not touched:** `risk/` (per constraint), broker config, `AUTO_TRADE`, any live path.

## 4. Recommendation / handoff to next instance

1. **Do not scale risk, add leverage, or widen the universe** to make a no-edge strategy
   look profitable. That is how paper losses become real ones.
2. **Next concrete task:** run the *full* filtered Config C (`run_walkforward_v2.py`,
   now cost-aware) on the full 30-symbol universe and record the **net** aggregate here.
   Needs `yfinance` + `pyarrow` installed (this session installed them in the ephemeral
   container; they are not committed). If net < SPY (expected), that is the decisive,
   like-for-like number.
3. **Then decide direction honestly:**
   - The daily-bar backtest cannot test the strategy's actual intraday edge (VWAP,
     first-hour, 5m MTF). If anyone still believes there's an edge, the *only* fair test
     is a **cost-inclusive intraday backtest** (`backtesting/engine_intraday.py`, which
     also has no cost model yet). Until that exists, claims of an intraday edge are
     unfalsified, not validated.
   - If a cost-inclusive intraday test also fails to beat SPY, **recommend winding down
     the trading thesis** and being explicit that buy-and-hold (or a simple trend-following
     allocation) dominates.
4. **Separate the two projects in your head:** "make the bot's trading profitable" (the
   mandate) vs. "ship a trading SaaS." The recent commits serve the latter. Do not let
   SaaS polish be mistaken for trading-edge progress.
