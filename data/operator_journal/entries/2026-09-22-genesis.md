  "path to 100%" that leans on selling a signal service.

2. **Bootstrapped the operator infrastructure** — `MANDATE.md` (flagged

All work is additive (journal + docs). **`risk/` was not touched.**

## Evidence

Walk-forward, 2025-07-24 → 2026-09-21 (~201 trading days, 30-stock core list).
Log: `data/operator_journal/evidence/2026-09-22-walkforward-v1.txt`.

| Aggregate            | Baseline | Filtered (prod) | SPY buy&hold |
|----------------------|----------|-----------------|--------------|
| Total return         | +4.75%   | **+2.97%**      | **+23.58%**  |
| Annualized (est.)    | ~6.0%    | **~3.7%**       | **~20%**     |
| Win rate             | 54.1%    | 45.1%           | —            |
| Profit factor        | 1.54     | 1.17            | —            |
| Trades               | 85       | 173             | 1            |

- **The RDT filter stack HURT:** −$446 (−1.78%) vs baseline, −9pt win rate,
  after filtering out 96.8% of signals. This contradicts the CLAUDE.md table
  claiming filters improve returns ($815 → $1,716). Did not replicate.
- **No transaction costs modeled anywhere** (`engine.py`:
  `pnl = (exit − entry) × shares`, fills exactly at target/stop). 3.7% is an
  optimistic ceiling; 173 real round-trips would erode it further.
- The repo's own `ACTIONABLE_100X_STRATEGY.md` states **Kelly ≈ −0.02
  (negative)** — a marginal-to-nonexistent edge, consistent with the above.

## Verdict

**Not profitable against the mandate's bar.** Net of honest costs, over a
reproducible out-of-sample window, the production strategy returned ~3.7%
(gross, optimistic) vs SPY's ~23.6%. It **underperformed SPY buy-and-hold by
~17–20 percentage points** while taking 173 trades and carrying real drawdown.
The signature is overfitting: the "RDT methodology" filters that define the
product lose money on unseen data.

Caveat for honesty: this window was an unusually strong bull (SPY +23.6%), the
worst environment for a long/flat momentum system that sits in cash 96% of the
time. But the strategy's *own* best-case (~6.8%) and negative Kelly mean it
would trail SPY's long-run ~10% in normal conditions too, and adding more risk
cannot fix a negative-Kelly edge.

## Recommendation

**Escalate to the human owner with a decision, not more feature work.** Ranked:

1. **Stop optimizing the RRS filter stack.** Fresh evidence says it subtracts
   value. Further tuning will overfit harder.
2. **Make the backtester honest before trusting any future number:** add a
   commission + slippage model to `backtesting/engine.py` and re-baseline. Any
   strategy that isn't profitable *after* costs is not a strategy. (This is the
   single highest-leverage code change and the natural next session's task.)
3. **Re-run the decision on a cost-aware, multi-regime window** (include a
   bear/flat period, not just this bull) to avoid judging on one lucky/unlucky
   tape. If profit factor after costs isn't clearly > 1.1 out-of-sample, treat
   the edge as absent.
4. **If it still fails: wind the trading ambition down.** The defensible
   position is SPY (or a low-cost index) buy-and-hold; keep this repo as a
   dashboard/paper-research platform, not an alpha engine. Do **not** ship a
   paid signal service built on a negative-Kelly strategy.

Next operator session (proposed): implement cost modeling (#2), re-run the
3-way comparison net of costs across a 2-year multi-regime window, and record
whether *any* configuration clears profit-factor > 1.1 after costs.
