## TL;DR / Verdict

Over the repository's **own** walk-forward backtest engine, across the most
recent ~2 years (2024-07-12 → 2026-07-24, 510 trading days, $25K per window):

| Config (gross, zero-cost) | Total Return | Annualized | Trades | Risk-adj (Ret/MaxDD) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A) Baseline (no filters) | +7.43% | 3.7% | 234 | **2.86 (best)** |
| B) Old Filters | +5.06% | 2.5% | 427 | 1.37 |
| C) RDT Filters (current prod) | +7.68% | 3.8% | 280 | 2.76 |
| **SPY buy-and-hold (benchmark)** | **+35.08%** | **+16.02%** | 1 | — |

**The strategy does not beat SPY buy-and-hold. It is not close.** SPY returned
**+35%** over the identical window; the best strategy config returned **+7.7%
gross** and trails SPY by **~12 percentage points per year even before costs.**

Net of honest transaction costs the picture worsens:

| Per round-trip cost | A) Baseline | C) RDT Filters |
|---|---|---|
| $2 | +5.55% | +5.44% |
| $4 | +3.68% | +3.20% |
| $6 | +1.81% | +0.96% |
| $8 | **−0.06%** | **−1.28%** |
| $10 | −1.93% | −3.52% |

Best config C vs SPY, net, annualized: **+2.65%/yr @ $2 → −0.63%/yr @ $8**, a
gap of **−13 to −17 pts/yr vs SPY** in every scenario. The "Old Filters" config
(B) is the worst — more trades, lower return — and goes negative at ~$3/trade.

**Two structural findings:**
1. The elaborate RDT filter stack (C) beats the **no-filter baseline (A)** by
   only **+0.25% / +$63 over six windows**, and A has the better risk-adjusted
   return. The filters are not earning their complexity.
2. The system's performance claims rest on a backtest that applied **zero
   transaction costs** and **never benchmarked SPY**. Both gaps are now closed
   (see code changes). This is the first honest apples-to-apples measurement in
   the repo's history.

## Honest caveats (do not oversell this verdict)

- **Bull-market window.** 2024-07 → 2026-07 was strongly up (SPY +35%). A
  long-biased stock picker competing with raw beta faces a high bar here. But
  the mission's stated benchmark *is* SPY B&H, so this is the relevant test.
- **The strategy's one genuine edge is drawdown.** Max DD was ~$650–920
  (2.6–3.7%) vs SPY's far deeper intra-period drawdowns. On a pure
  capital-preservation / Sharpe basis the gap is less brutal. But net-of-cost
  absolute return is what the mandate names, and there it loses decisively.
- **Backtest optimism remains** even after costs: fills are modeled at the daily
  close; no partial-fill, gap, or liquidity modeling. Real results would likely
  be *worse*, not better.
- One engine, one 30-symbol large-cap universe, one window. Strong signal, not
  the final word — but the margin (~13–17 pts/yr) is far too large to be noise.

## Recommendation

1. **Stop tuning filters.** The no-filter baseline already matches/beats the
   full RDT filter stack; the 180-combo optimization behind the "100X" docs is
   overfitting. Incremental filter work is very unlikely to close a 12+ pt/yr
   gross gap.
2. **Reframe or escalate.** Two honest paths:
   - **(a) Change the yardstick deliberately.** If the product's real value is
     *lower-drawdown absolute return* rather than beating SPY, say that openly
     and measure it that way (Sharpe, Calmar, max DD) — but it must still clear
     costs, which at $6+/round-trip it barely does.
   - **(b) Test the falsifying case.** Run the same honest pipeline over a
     bear/sideways span (e.g. 2022) where a defensive, low-exposure strategy
     could plausibly *beat* B&H. If it cannot win even there, that is strong
     grounds for **wind-down of active strategy development.**
3. **Do not expand capital or risk** on this strategy based on the existing
   marketing docs; their numbers are not net-of-cost and not SPY-benchmarked.
