## State of the evidence

**1. Net-of-cost P&L vs SPY — the strategy loses badly to the benchmark.**

| | Best documented config (RDT filters) | SPY buy-and-hold |
|---|---|---|
| Window | ~2 yr (Feb 2024 – Nov 2025, per CLAUDE.md) | 2 yr (Aug 2024 – Aug 2026, IBKR) |
| Total return | +6.9% | **+35.97%** (563.68 → 766.43) |
| Annualized | **~3.4% (gross, no costs)** | **~16.6%** |
| Costs modeled | **None** (see point 3) | n/a (1 trade) |

SPY benchmark independently verified via IBKR MCP `get_price_history` /
`get_price_snapshot` (contract 756733): 1-yr **+19.44%**, 3-yr **+69.34%**
(~19.2% CAGR), YTD-2026 +10.09%. An investor would have made **~5x more money
doing nothing but holding SPY**, with far less operational risk.

**2. The strategy's own math says there is no edge.**
`ACTIONABLE_100X_STRATEGY.md` documents: win rate 38%, profit factor 1.29,
win/loss ratio 1.55 → **Kelly = −0.02 (negative)**. A negative Kelly means
increasing size increases risk of ruin without improving expectancy. The doc's own
conclusion is that trading alone can't hit the goal and pivots to selling a signal
*service* — i.e. it concedes the trading edge.

**3. The backtest is frictionless — the 3.4% is an overstatement.**
`grep` for `commission|slippage|fee|spread|cost` in `engine_enhanced.py`,
`engine.py`, `engine_intraday.py` returns **nothing**. Exits fill at the exact
`target_price` / `stop_price` with no slippage or gap modeling. At ~279 trades over
2 years, realistic per-trade friction (commission + half-spread on entry and exit)
would erode a large fraction of a 3.4% gross return. **Net of honest costs, the
edge is plausibly at or below zero.**

**4. There is essentially no real track record.**
`signal_metrics.json`: 880 scans, 120 signals, but **only 2 closed outcomes**
(1 target hit, 1 stop out). Two trades is statistically meaningless. Also notable:
**119 short signals vs 1 long signal** — a momentum/long-biased "RealDayTrading"
system emitting ~99% shorts suggests either a heavily bearish sample window or a
filter/logic skew worth investigating, but either way there is no evidence of live
profitability.

   `tests/test_backtest_costs.py` (4 tests, all passing) locking in the formula,

   frictionless-equivalence escape hatch.

### Rough cost impact (estimate, pending a full net-of-cost re-run)

The best documented config (RDT filters) made **~$1,716 gross** over 2 years on
**279 trades**. At the new default cost assumptions, round-trip cost is on the
order of **$7–12 per trade** (≈$1–2 commission + ~10 bps slippage on a rough
$7–10k average notional), i.e. **~$2,000–3,300 in total costs** — *comparable to
or exceeding the entire gross profit.* This strongly implies the **net-of-cost
return is near zero or negative.** This is an estimate, not a computed backtest;
a full net-of-cost walk-forward re-run (blocked here by yfinance) is needed to
confirm the exact figure, but it reinforces, not weakens, the verdict.
