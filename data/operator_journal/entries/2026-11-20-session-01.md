## 3. Measured results (net of costs, vs SPY buy-and-hold)

### Bull window — 2024-07-26 → 2026-08-07 (~510 trading days), SPY total return +45%

| Config | Net return | Ann. | PF | Txn costs | Trades |
|--------|-----------|------|------|-----------|--------|
| A) Baseline    | +\$1,546 (+6.18%) | 3.1%/yr | 1.24 | \$500 | 229 |
| B) Old Filters | +\$1,440 (+5.76%) | 2.8%/yr | 1.14 | \$804 | 419 |
| C) RDT Filters | **+\$2,231 (+8.93%)** | **4.4%/yr** | 1.31 | \$567 | 268 |
| **SPY B&H (apples-to-apples)** | **+\$9,451 (+37.80%)** | ~18%/yr | — | — | 1 |

→ Best config (C) **loses to SPY by \$7,219 (−28.88 points).** It captured ~24% of what
doing nothing captured. (Single full-span SPY hold: +\$11,317 / +45.27%.)

### Bear/flat window — 2021-10-05 → 2023-10-13 (incl. the 2022 crash), SPY total return ~+5.75%

| Config | Net return | Ann. | PF | Txn costs | Trades |
|--------|-----------|------|------|-----------|--------|
| A) Baseline    | **+\$1,430 (+5.72%)** | 2.8%/yr | 1.41 | \$296 | 122 |
| B) Old Filters | −\$662 (−2.65%) | −1.3%/yr | 0.86 | \$373 | 185 |
| C) RDT Filters | −\$488 (−1.95%) | −1.0%/yr | 0.87 | \$280 | 129 |
| **SPY B&H (apples-to-apples)** | **+\$1,437 (+5.75%)** | ~2.8%/yr | — | — | 1 |

→ Best config (A, raw baseline) **ties SPY** (−\$7.58, a rounding error). The filtered
configs (B, C) **lose money outright** — profit factor < 1.

### The two findings that matter

- **No config, in either regime, net of costs, beats SPY buy-and-hold.** Best case = tie.
- **The RDT filter stack is not an edge; in the bear regime it is a liability.** It
  converted a +5.72% / PF-1.41 baseline into a −1.95% / PF-0.87 loss. This directly
  **falsifies** the CLAUDE.md thesis that the filters yield "fewer but higher-quality
  trades." They yield fewer, *worse*-in-aggregate trades once the market stops trending up.
  (In the bull run C did edge out A — but both lost to SPY by a mile, so "best of the
  losers" is not a win.)

## 4. Honest steelman (what could rescue the strategy — and why it doesn't)

- *"It's a bull-market artifact; momentum sits in cash and can't beat a melt-up."* Tested.
  In the flat/bear window SPY only made +5.75%, a beatable bar — and the strategy still
  only tied it, with the filters losing money. The bar was low and it was not cleared.
- *"Lower drawdown is worth lower return."* The baseline's max DD was ~2.9% of \$25k vs
  SPY's ~−25% in 2022 — genuinely lower. But that is mostly because the strategy is barely
  deployed (near-100% cash). A 20% SPY / 80% cash blend would have ~1/5 the drawdown AND
  (over the bull window) still crushed the strategy's return. "Low drawdown via mostly
  holding cash" is not an edge; it is under-investment.
- *"Scale up the per-trade risk to match SPY's exposure."* That scales the thin PF-1.2–1.3
  edge, the costs, AND the drawdowns proportionally; it does not create alpha. A 1.2–1.4
  profit factor on a near-50% win rate is exactly the kind of razor-thin edge that costs
  are designed to eat — which is what the bear-window filtered configs show (PF < 1 after
  costs).
